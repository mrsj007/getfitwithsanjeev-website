What is BMR?

Your basal metabolic rate (BMR) is the energy your body uses in a day at complete rest, just to keep you alive. It powers your heart and lungs, keeps your body temperature steady, and fuels your brain, kidneys and the constant repair of cells. For most people, BMR is the largest part of the calories they burn each day, often well over half. You cannot feel it working, and it carries on whether you train or not.

BMR is measured in kilocalories (kcal) a day. On food labels in Canada, "Calories" means the same thing. A BMR calculator like the one above does not measure anything. It uses research formulas to estimate your BMR from your age, sex, height and weight, and from body fat if you know it.

BMR vs TDEE vs resting metabolic rate

These three terms are easy to mix up. BMR is measured in a lab under strict conditions: lying still, after a night's sleep and an overnight fast, in a comfortable room. Resting metabolic rate (RMR), also called resting energy expenditure, is measured under looser conditions, so it usually comes out a little higher than BMR. In everyday use the two are close enough that most calculators, including this one, treat them as roughly the same number.

TDEE, or total daily energy expenditure, is everything you burn in a day: your BMR, plus the energy used to digest food (often estimated at about 10 percent of what you eat), plus all your movement, from walking the dog and carrying groceries to a training session. TDEE is your estimated maintenance calories: roughly what you would eat in a day to keep your weight steady.

How to calculate BMR by hand

The calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor formula, published in 1990 and widely used by dietitians:

  • Women: BMR = 10 x weight (kg) + 6.25 x height (cm) - 5 x age (years) - 161
  • Men: BMR = 10 x weight (kg) + 6.25 x height (cm) - 5 x age (years) + 5

Worked example. A 30-year-old woman who is 165 cm tall and weighs 65 kg:

Worked example of the Mifflin-St Jeor formula
StepSumResult
Weight10 x 65650
Height6.25 x 1651,031.25
Age5 x 30- 150
Sex adjustmentFemale- 161
BMR650 + 1,031.25 - 150 - 1611,370 kcal a day

If she is moderately active, her TDEE is 1,370 x 1.55, or about 2,120 kcal a day. Using imperial units? Multiply pounds by 0.4536 to get kilograms and inches by 2.54 to get centimetres first.

If you know your body fat percentage from a reliable scan, the Katch-McArdle formula is another option: BMR = 370 + 21.6 x lean body mass (kg). Lean body mass is your weight minus your fat. For someone who weighs 80 kg at 20 percent body fat, lean mass is 64 kg, so BMR = 370 + 21.6 x 64 = about 1,750 kcal a day. Activity multipliers

To estimate TDEE, multiply your BMR by the factor that best matches a normal week. Most people overestimate their activity, so if you are between two levels, start with the lower one.

What affects BMR

Body size is the biggest factor: a larger body has more tissue to keep running. Muscle uses more energy at rest than fat does. Age matters because most adults slowly lose muscle and become less active over the decades, unless they train for it. Sex matters mostly because, on average, men carry more muscle at the same weight. Genetics means two people with identical numbers can still differ. Hormones, including thyroid function and the changes around menopause, can shift energy use, and so can illness, fever, some medications and long periods of very low eating. If you notice big, unexplained changes in weight or energy, see your doctor.

BMR and muscle

You will often read that strength training "boosts your metabolism". The honest version is smaller. Muscle does burn more energy at rest than fat, so adding muscle nudges your BMR up a little, but a few kilograms of new muscle changes your daily total by a modest amount, not hundreds of calories. The real reasons to strength train are bigger: you get stronger, you protect your bones and joints, you stay steady on your feet, and when you eat a little less to lose fat, training helps you keep the muscle you have. Two or three sessions a week is enough to start. Read more in Sanjeev's muscle gain guidance and strength training articles.

How many calories to eat

Start with maintenance, your TDEE. Eat around that number for two or three weeks and watch the trend in your weight, energy, sleep and training. If you want to lose fat gradually, a gentle step about 10 to 20 percent below maintenance is a common starting point. To build muscle, a small step of about 5 to 10 percent above maintenance is usually enough. Whatever your goal, a target on this page never goes below your BMR, because eating that little tends to leave people tired, hungry and losing muscle.

These are general starting ranges, not a meal plan. This page does not suggest macros, meal plans or promises like "eat this to lose a kilogram a week". If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have a medical condition, take medication that affects weight or appetite, or if thinking about food or weight feels stressful, talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian before changing how you eat. For habits that last, see Fat Loss Without the Crash.

BMR for older adults

BMR tends to drift down with age, but much of that comes from losing muscle and moving less, and both can be slowed with regular strength and balance work. For adults 65 and over, this calculator shows maintenance only and does not suggest a fat-loss range: losing weight later in life can mean losing muscle, which makes everyday tasks harder. If you are thinking about losing weight at this stage, speak with your doctor first. Sanjeev's guidance on arthritis, osteoporosis and sarcopenia and strength training after 50 is a good next read.

BMR with medication or health conditions

Thyroid conditions, diabetes, heart and kidney conditions, recovery from illness or surgery, and medications such as steroids, some antidepressants, GLP-1 medications like Ozempic and hormone therapy can all change appetite, weight or energy use. A formula cannot account for any of that. If any of these apply to you, use your result only as background for a conversation, and talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian before changing how you eat. Never change a medication because of a calculator.

How accurate is a BMR calculator?

Formulas give estimates. In a 2005 review comparing common equations, Mifflin-St Jeor was the most reliable, coming within about 10 percent of measured resting energy for many healthy adults. That still leaves some people further off, especially those with a lot of muscle, very high or low body fat, or health conditions. The activity multiplier adds more uncertainty, because a "moderately active" week means different things to different people. That is why the result is rounded to the nearest 10 kcal and labelled "about". Use it as a starting point, then let a few weeks of real life tell you whether to adjust.

What to do with your result

Pick one small habit, not a whole new life. If your goal is fat loss, start near maintenance or the top of your range, add two or three strength sessions a week and walk most days; fat and weight loss nutrition notes and fat loss training articles explain the basics. If you want to build muscle, eat around your range and follow a simple progressive plan. If you are maintaining, keep training, protein and sleep steady.
