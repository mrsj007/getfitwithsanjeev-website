If you spend most of your day at a desk, you probably know the feeling: stiff hips when you stand up, tight shoulders, a sore neck by the afternoon. It is tempting to blame your chair or your posture. The reality is simpler. Our bodies are built to change position, and a day spent in one shape leaves some joints under-used.

The good news is that you do not need a long routine to feel better. You need small, regular movement and a handful of exercises that target the areas desk work tends to stiffen.

## Sitting is not the villain

Sitting is a normal position. The trouble comes when it is the only one, for hours at a time. Rather than hunting for a "perfect" posture, aim for variety: sit, stand, walk, shift your weight, and change your position often.

## Movement snacks beat one big session

A 60-second break every 30 to 60 minutes does more for stiffness than a long stretch once a day. Try:

- Stand up and walk to refill your water.
- Take phone calls on your feet when you can.
- Set a gentle timer and do two or three of the movements below.

## Where desk workers tend to feel stiff

- **Hips.** Long sitting keeps the hips bent, which can make the fronts of your hips feel tight.
- **Upper back.** Rounding forward over a keyboard reduces how much the upper back rotates and extends.
- **Shoulders and neck.** Arms reaching forward and a head held ahead of the body can leave this area tired.

## A 10-minute mobility routine

Do each move slowly, breathing normally. Stay in a comfortable range. You are looking for a gentle stretch or a feeling of movement, never pain.

1. **Walking warm-up, 1 minute.** Walk around the room, swinging your arms.
2. **Cat-cow, 8 slow reps.** On hands and knees, round your back, then lower your belly and lift your chest. It wakes up the spine.
3. **Half-kneeling hip stretch, 30 to 45 seconds each side.** Kneel on one knee with the other foot forward. Tuck your pelvis slightly and gently move your hips forward until you feel a stretch at the front of the back hip.
4. **Thoracic rotation, 8 reps each side.** On hands and knees, place one hand behind your head and turn your chest toward the ceiling, then back.
5. **Wall slides, 8 to 10 reps.** Stand with your back near a wall, arms bent, and slide your arms up and down without arching your lower back.
6. **Hip hinge, 8 to 10 reps.** Push your hips back with a soft knee bend, keep your back long, then stand tall. It teaches your hips to do the bending instead of your lower back.
7. **Glute bridge, 10 reps.** Lie on your back, knees bent, and lift your hips. This strengthens the muscles that sitting tends to switch off.
8. **Neck and shoulder reset, 1 minute.** Gently drop one ear toward the shoulder, hold briefly, and repeat on the other side. Roll your shoulders back a few times.

You can split these into two or three mini-sessions across the day.

## Strength helps mobility last

Stretching alone can feel good, but mobility sticks better when you pair it with strength. If your muscles can control a new range of movement, your body is more likely to keep it. Simple exercises like sit-to-stands, rows and carries are a good start.

## Small changes to your workday

- Raise your screen so your eyes are near the top third of it.
- Keep frequently used items within easy reach to avoid over-stretching.
- Use a laptop stand or an external keyboard if you work from a laptop.
- Take a short walk at lunch, even 10 minutes.

## When to get it checked

Tight and stiff is common. Numbness, tingling, pain that travels into an arm or leg, weakness, or pain that keeps getting worse needs a professional opinion. This article is general education and does not replace advice from your doctor or physiotherapist.
